**Gross margin is not a relevant metric for JPMorgan Chase.** Its 2022 Form 10-K never uses "gross margin," "gross profit," or "cost of goods sold," and the income statement has no revenue-minus-COGS structure.

JPM is a bank, not a manufacturer: revenue is net interest income (interest earned on loans/securities minus interest paid on deposits) plus noninterest revenue (fees, trading, asset management). Its expenses—compensation, technology, credit-loss provisions—are not costs of goods sold, so no gross-profit line exists.

Closest substitutes move with rates and credit cycles: net interest income jumped 28% in 2022 ($52.3B→$66.7B), the credit-loss provision swung $17.5B→−$9.3B→$6.4B, and the expense ratio (noninterest expense÷total net revenue) ran 55.6%→58.6%→59.2%—beyond a 2-point band.
