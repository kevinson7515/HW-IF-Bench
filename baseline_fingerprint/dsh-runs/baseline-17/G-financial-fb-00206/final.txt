I've extracted and reviewed JPMorgan's 2022 Form 10-K income statement directly from SEC EDGAR. Here's my answer.

## Short answer

**Gross margin is not a relevant (or reported) metric for JPMorgan Chase — and that is the correct answer to your question, not a dodge.** There is no "gross margin" to assess for historical consistency, because the metric doesn't exist on a bank's financial statements.

## Why gross margin doesn't apply here

Gross margin is defined as **(revenue − cost of goods sold) ÷ revenue**. It only carries meaning when a company has a *direct cost of revenue* — the incremental cost of producing each unit of a physical product (raw materials, direct labor, manufacturing). That structure simply doesn't exist for a bank:

- JPMorgan doesn't manufacture or sell goods; it earns revenue from **net interest income** (the spread between interest earned on loans/securities and interest paid on deposits/borrowings) and **noninterest revenue** (fees, trading/principal transactions, investment banking, asset-management fees, commissions).
- A bank's main operating costs — **compensation, occupancy, technology, professional services** — are *operating* expenses, not a "cost of goods sold." You can't meaningfully map a per-unit product cost onto a loan or a deposit.

I confirmed this in the filing itself: the 2022 10-K contains **zero** occurrences of "gross margin," "gross profit," "cost of goods," "cost of revenue," or "cost of sales."

There is no "gross profit" subtotal anywhere between "Total net revenue" and the expense lines — revenue flows straight into the provision for credit losses and noninterest expense.

## The metrics that *are* the bank equivalents of "gross margin"

If you're looking for the bank analog of a top-line profitability/consistency measure, the standard ones are:

1. **Net interest margin (NIM)** — net interest income ÷ average interest-earning assets (the "cost of funding" analog).
2. **Overhead / efficiency ratio** — total noninterest expense ÷ total net revenue. JPMorgan reports this directly, and it's been notably stable: **59% / 59% / 56%** for 2022 / 2021 / 2020.
3. **Pre-provision profit (PPNR)** and **pre-tax margin** — but note these *do* swing year-to-year for a bank because of the **provision for credit losses** (a reserve-building/credit-cost line with no product-company equivalent), not because of any "gross margin" volatility.

## One caveat

If you've seen a "gross margin" figure for JPM on a third-party stock-data website, treat it with skepticism: it's a **non-standard synthetic calculation** that a data vendor derived (for example, treating interest expense as a proxy for "cost of revenue"), not a number JPMorgan reports or manages to. It's not GAAP and isn't a meaningful way to evaluate a bank.

**Bottom line:** The question "are JPM's gross margins historically consistent within ~2%/year?" has no direct answer, because gross margin is not a relevant metric for a deposit-taking financial institution. The closest meaningful proxy — the overhead/efficiency ratio — has been stable (56–59% over 2020–2022).
