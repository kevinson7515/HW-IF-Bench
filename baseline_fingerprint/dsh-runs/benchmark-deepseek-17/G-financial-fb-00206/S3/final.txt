## Answer

Gross margin is **not a relevant metric for JPMorgan Chase**, and the company does not report it. You should not evaluate JPM on gross margin — the metric does not apply to its business model.

**Why gross margin doesn't apply**

Gross margin is defined as `(Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold) / Revenue`. It is meaningful only for companies that sell tangible goods or deliver services with a directly matched, per-unit "cost of goods sold" (manufacturers, retailers, etc.). JPMorgan Chase is a bank and financial-services firm, not a goods producer, so it has **no cost of goods sold** and therefore no gross margin line.

That absence is confirmed in the filing itself: a full-text search of the 382-page 2022 Form 10-K finds **zero occurrences** of "gross margin," "gross profit," "cost of goods sold," or "cost of revenue." The concept simply isn't part of a bank's financial reporting.

**How a bank's income statement is structured instead**

JPMorgan's top line is built from two components, neither of which has an associated "COGS":

- **Net interest income** — interest earned on loans and securities *minus* interest paid on deposits and borrowings (the interest expense is already netted out, not shown as "cost of goods").
- **Noninterest revenue** — fees, commissions, investment banking, trading, asset management, card income, etc.

Total net revenue = Net interest income + Noninterest revenue. Below that, the main cost lines are **noninterest expense** (compensation, occupancy, technology, marketing) and **provision for credit losses** — none of which are a "cost of goods sold."

**The metrics banks use instead**

If you want the banking analog of "profitability before operating costs," the relevant measures are:

- **Net interest margin / net yield on interest-earning assets** — the banking equivalent of a spread on the balance sheet. JPMorgan reported a net yield of **2.00%** (FTE basis) in 2022, up 36 bps.
- **Overhead ratio** (noninterest expense ÷ total net revenue) — reported at **59% (2022), 59% (2021), 56% (2020)**.
- **Pre-provision profit margin** and **return on equity/ROTCE** — the standard profitability gauges for a bank.

So the premise of the question — checking whether JPM's gross margins fluctuate more than ~2% year over year — can't be tested, because the metric doesn't exist for a bank. That is not a data limitation; it reflects the fundamental structure of a bank's business.
