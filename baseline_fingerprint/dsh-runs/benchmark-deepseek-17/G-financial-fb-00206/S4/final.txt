Gross margins are **not a relevant metric** for JPMorgan Chase. It is a bank holding company, so its income statement contains no "cost of goods sold" and therefore no "gross profit" — the two inputs needed to compute gross margin (Revenue − COGS ÷ Revenue). Instead, the 10-K reports Total net revenue, Net interest income (interest income minus interest expense), and Total noninterest expense. The firm's costs are funding costs plus operating expenses (compensation, technology, occupancy), not COGS. Thus there is no "gross margin" series to test for historical stability.

The closest bank-specific analogue is net interest margin (net yield on interest-earning assets), which was 2.00% in 2022 — but that is not a gross margin.
