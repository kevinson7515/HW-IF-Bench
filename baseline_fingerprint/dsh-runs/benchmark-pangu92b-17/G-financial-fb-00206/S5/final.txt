No — gross margin is **not a relevant metric** for JPMorgan Chase, so the "historically consistent within ~2%" framing doesn't apply.

**Why:** Gross margin = revenue − cost of goods sold. JPMorgan is a bank, not a manufacturer/retailer, so it has **no cost of goods sold**. Its income statement (2022 10-K, p. 159) contains no COGS line; instead it flows:

- Noninterest revenue + Net interest income = Total net revenue
- Provision for credit losses
- Noninterest expense (compensation, occupancy, technology, etc.)
- Income before tax → Net income

Its "products" are financial intermediation (loans, deposits, trading, fees), so the relevant profitability measures are different:

- Net interest margin / net yield on interest-earning assets (e.g., 2.00% managed basis in 2022)
- Overhead ratio (noninterest expense ÷ total net revenue; 59%, 59%, 56% for 2022–2020)
- ROE / ROTCE (14%/18% in 2022)

These are the metrics JPMorgan actually discloses and manages to — not gross margin.
