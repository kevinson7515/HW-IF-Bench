Technical Issues That Grounded Starliner

Shortly after launch, helium leaks and propulsion malfunctions became evident. The spacecraft, which had already faced years of delays and mishaps, encountered thrust failures, with five thrusters ceasing to function mid-mission.

Engineers on Earth replicated Starliner's propulsion system to determine the root cause. Their tests revealed that unexpected heat buildup caused Teflon seals to bulge, restricting propellant flow to the thrusters.

NASA's Safety Concerns Overruled Boeing

Despite Boeing's efforts to troubleshoot the issue, NASA remained deeply concerned about the safety of flying Starliner back to Earth. By August 2024, the space agency officially ruled out the option of using Starliner for reentry, stranding Williams and Wilmore in space until an alternative return mission was arranged.

What's Next for Starliner?

While the Starliner capsule returned uncrewed in September, proving it could safely land, it remains uncertain when—or if—Boeing's spacecraft will fly astronauts again.
