Olivia Munn is having none of Blue Origin's New Shepard NS-31 mission.

In case you missed it, private space company Blue Origin (owned by Jeff Bezos) will be conducting an all-female flight to space — depressingly, the first all-woman crew since Russian astronaut Valentina Tereshkova went to space by herself in 1963. Crew members include Katy Perry, Gayle King, and Jeff's fiancée Lauren Sánchez. They'll be hurtled up to space on April 14.

There are some caveats: The rocket will fly itself. The total trip will only last about 11 minutes, with around four of those floating in space. The crew have also spoken about donning full "glam" for the occasion, such as eyelash glue, apparently making them the first people to have their hair and makeup done while going up to space.

In a recent episode of TODAY with Jenna & Friends, Olivia reacted to the news and asked a question many of us are thinking: "Why? I know this probably is not the cool thing to say, but there are so many other things that are so important in the world right now. There's one astronaut. What are you guys going to do up in space?"

Comparing the 11-minute flight duration to a rollercoaster, she continued, "If you wanna go up to space, why do you need to tell us about it?"

"I know this is probably obnoxious, but like, it's so much money to go to space, and there's a lot of people who can't even afford eggs," she continued, later noting the amount of "resources being spent" on the trip.

When Jenna Bush Hager noted the women's words on traveling in full glam, Olivia reacted, "They said this out loud?!" She added, "What's the point? Is it historic that you guys are going on a ride? I think it's a bit gluttonous."

The 44-year-old continued, "Space exploration was to further our knowledge and to help mankind. What are they gonna do up there that has made it better for us down here?"

"I'm not saying I hope they get stuck up there, but wouldn't that be fun? Just for a little bit," she joked. "They will be fine. Jeff Bezos owns everything. First of all, they're not going to get stuck. Second of all, they're not really going up to space."
